Key Research Papers — Direct Download Links

⚠ Access Note: Papers marked OPEN ACCESS can be downloaded directly for free. DOI papers require institutional access or a request via ResearchGate/Sci-Hub. Click the link button to go directly to each paper.
✓ OPEN ACCESS
Study on the Relationship between Groundwater and Land Subsidence in Bangladesh Combining GRACE and InSAR
Ouyang L., Zhao Z., Zhou D. et al. · Remote Sensing (MDPI) · Vol. 16, No. 19 · October 2024 · DOI: 10.3390/rs16193715
This is the most directly relevant paper to your topic. It uses GRACE + GLDAS data merged to analyze groundwater storage changes across Bangladesh, while SBAS-InSAR technology assesses land subsidence. Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) is applied to find correlations between groundwater time series and subsidence patterns. Precipitation and land cover data are incorporated.
KEY FINDING → Groundwater storage in Bangladesh is declining at −5.55 mm/year on average. The worst declines are in Rangpur, Mymensingh, and Rajshahi divisions. The SBAS-InSAR time series uses free LiCSAR Sentinel-1 interferograms, corrected with GACOS atmospheric products.
DOWNLOAD PDF — mdpi.com
✓ OPEN ACCESS
Land Subsidence Monitoring Using InSAR Technique in the Southwestern Region of Bangladesh
Multiple Authors · Geomatics, Natural Hazards and Risk · Taylor & Francis · April 2024 · DOI: 10.1080/19475705.2024.2333795
Employs InSAR analysis spanning 2014–2022 to measure and spatially visualize land subsidence in the Ganges-Brahmaputra Delta (GBD) region of southwestern Bangladesh. Uses Sentinel-1 SAR data for the entire period. Combines sediment compaction, tectonic factors, and human activity analysis.
KEY FINDING → Subsidence rates range from 3–20 mm/year, with most areas experiencing 3–10 mm/year. The southwest region shows the highest depletion rate.
ACCESS PAPER — tandfonline.com
✓ OPEN ACCESS
Subsidence in the Kathmandu Basin, Before and After the 2015 Mw 7.8 Gorkha Earthquake, Nepal Revealed from SBAS-DInSAR Analysis
Multiple Authors · GIScience & Remote Sensing · Taylor & Francis · 2018 · DOI: 10.1080/15481603.2017.1422312
Directly addresses Nepal's Kathmandu Basin using SBAS-DInSAR with 16 ALOS-1 PALSAR images (pre-seismic) and 26 Sentinel-1 A/B SAR images (pre/post-earthquake). Groundwater level data from wells show a significant decline from 2000–2014, correlated with subsidence zones.
KEY FINDING → InSAR LOS deformation from Sentinel-1A shows strong correlation with GPS station measurements. Groundwater depletion is identified as the dominant driver of anthropogenic subsidence in the Kathmandu valley.
ACCESS PAPER — tandfonline.com
✓ OPEN ACCESS
Global Land Subsidence Mapping Reveals Widespread Loss of Aquifer Storage Capacity
Hasan M.F., Smith R., Vajedian S. et al. · Nature Communications · Vol. 14 · October 2023 · DOI: 10.1038/s41467-023-41933-z
Develops a machine learning approach to map subsidence globally using InSAR observations as training data. The paper explores subsidence drivers and identifies regions under high groundwater stress including South Asia. Highly cited and methodologically relevant for AI-based prediction.
KEY FINDING → InSAR provides ~1 cm accuracy at ~100 m spatial resolution. The ML model generalizes subsidence predictions to data-sparse regions like Nepal and Bangladesh.
DOWNLOAD — nature.com (Open Access)
✓ OPEN ACCESS
Monitoring Groundwater Storage Basins and Hydrological Changes in the Indo-Gangetic Basin Using InSAR and GRACE
ISPRS Archives · ASPRS Annual Conference 2023 · Denver, CO · DOI: 10.5194/isprs-archives-XLVIII-M-3-2023-95-2023
Covers the full Indo-Gangetic Plain which includes southern Nepal and virtually all of Bangladesh. Uses Sentinel-1 InSAR and GRACE satellite pair for characterizing hydrological dynamics. Combines U-Net deep learning architecture with InSAR and GRACE time series.
KEY FINDING → Around 1 meter of subsidence occurred in parts of Northern India between 2018 and 2023. InSAR time series reveals coherent seasonal uplift–subsidence cycles matching monsoon hydrology.
DOWNLOAD PDF — copernicus.org
✓ OPEN ACCESS
Groundwater Under the Lens: A Remote Sensing Exploration of Storage Patterns and Trends in Bangladesh
Multiple Authors · Journal of Water and Climate Change · IWA Publishing · 2025
Uses GRACE and GLDAS data to study spatial and temporal groundwater storage patterns across all regions of Bangladesh. Mann–Kendall trend tests reveal significant depletion gradients. Machine learning algorithms are reviewed for groundwater anomaly detection.
KEY FINDING → The southwestern region shows the highest depletion slope of −4.18 mm/year. The northeast shows a marginal increase in GWS. Overextraction is the primary driver.
ACCESS PAPER — iwaponline.com
DOI / ResearchGate
Interpreting Land Subsidence Impacts Due to Groundwater Depletion Using Remote Sensing-Based GRACE Gravity Anomaly and DInSAR Technique — NW India
ResearchGate · September 2021 · DOI: 10.1080/10106049.2022.2060322
Directly relevant for the Indo-Gangetic basin context covering Nepal and Bangladesh. Applies GRACE gravity anomaly time series combined with DInSAR to interpret subsidence caused by groundwater withdrawal in the transboundary aquifer system.
KEY FINDING → The IGB comprises 25% of global groundwater withdrawals. PS-InSAR results indicate over 60.9% of monitored points experienced subsidence, with some areas reaching 100 cm between 2014–2022.

04

Research Methodology Workflow

01
GRACE/GRACE-FO TWS Extraction
Download monthly Level-3 Mascon data (RL06 from JPL/CSR/GFZ). Apply glacial isostatic adjustment (GIA). Extract TWS anomalies over Nepal and Bangladesh grids.
02
GWS Isolation (GLDAS)
Download GLDAS-2.1 Noah model data. GWS = TWS – Soil Moisture – Snow Water Equivalent – Surface Water. This isolates groundwater storage change signal.
03
InSAR Processing (SBAS / PS)
Download Sentinel-1 SLC scenes. Process with SNAP + StaMPS / LiCSBAS / MintPy. Apply GACOS atmospheric correction. Generate displacement velocity maps (mm/year LOS).
04
Subsidence Mapping
Convert LOS displacement to vertical using incidence angle. Apply SBAS time series analysis. Identify subsidence bowls and hotspot zones at mm-scale resolution.
05
Correlation Analysis
Apply Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) or Pearson correlation between GWS time series and InSAR displacement. Incorporate borehole GWL data for validation.
06
AI Prediction Model
Train LSTM / Random Forest / XGBoost models on historical GRACE + InSAR + precipitation time series. Predict future groundwater depletion and subsidence rates.
07
Validation
Cross-validate InSAR results with GPS station data and in-situ well measurements. Validate GRACE GWS with published groundwater level reports from BWDB (Bangladesh) and DWRI (Nepal).
08
Risk Mapping
Overlay subsidence maps with population density, infrastructure, and flood-risk layers. Generate annual risk maps and future projection scenarios (2030, 2050).
05

AI & Machine Learning Applications

🧠
LSTM / GRU Networks
Long Short-Term Memory networks trained on GRACE monthly time series for multi-step groundwater storage forecasting. Captures seasonal monsoon patterns effectively.
🌲
Random Forest / XGBoost
Ensemble models for predicting subsidence magnitude using InSAR, GRACE, LULC, geology, and population density as features. Used in the Nature Communications global mapping study.
🖼
U-Net (Deep Learning)
Convolutional neural network (encoder-decoder) applied to SAR imagery for semantic segmentation of subsidence zones and flood mapping. Used in the ISPRS 2023 IGB study.
📐
DTW Similarity Analysis
Dynamic Time Warping quantifies the temporal similarity between GRACE GWS time series and InSAR subsidence time series even when signals are temporally offset.
📊
Mann-Kendall Trend Test
Non-parametric statistical method applied to GRACE TWS time series to detect statistically significant long-term depletion trends in each hydrological region.
🔗
Ensemble Data Fusion
Combining GRACE (large-scale gravity), InSAR (fine-scale deformation), GLDAS (soil moisture), and GPS/well data using Kalman-filter or ML ensemble approaches.
